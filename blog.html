DISCLAIMER: Every Manger/Human Recource/Interview person is going to be looking for certain things. What I cover below are some of the biggest mistakes I've seen made, but keep in mind, the person you're interviewing with may not even care about the below. At a minimum, do a little research on the company and position, review your resume, and go into the interview with confident humility, and you should be fine.

5 of the Worst Interview Habits

Lying During your Interview

I had a former NASA employee tell me in an interview that a critical alarm was going off and he was asked to find out why. Think of it like the O2 sensor was going below the acceptable level

So of course that peaked some follow up questions like what did you do and what did you find out? He gave a bunch of weak responses before he finally admitted . . .

O it quit alarming so we just moved on to the next problem.

So he was either lying about his involvement, which was a red flag, or he wasn't nearly as involved as he initially let on and was trying to take credit for someone elses work, which was also a red flag.

Knowing Nothing about the Company

Even if you give a wrong answer, that's 1000% better than giving no answer

Now let me clarify, you can't be completely wrong. If I work in a plant that makes Chemicals and you think you're interviewing for a company that makes Peanut Butter, it might be better to not say anything, BUT if I work for a company that makes Chemicals and you get the names wrong, or the process you explain isn't 100% correct, that's perfectly ok. At lease I know you're interested enough about the company to do some basic research beforehand.

Have at Least 1 ? for the Interviewer

Nothing screams I'm just interviewing with you to try and get an offer to counter for the job I really want as not having any questions at the end of the interview

I don't care if it's as easy as do you like your job or working for company X

You can also use it to learn more about what you're going to be working on if you ask what projects/jobs/responsibilities will I be assigned the first 6 months - 1 year I'll be working here?

I can't ever think of a time where nothing is better than something, and at least for me, even if the interview ran over a few minutes, I'll still make some time to answer at least one question.

Trying to Hard

As someone who graduated in the Spring of 2009 during the great financial crisis, I understand how bad you want a job. It took me almost 9 months to find my first job and applying to jobs for almost 9 months was my job.

BUT I have 2 perfectly good legs. I'm not hiring you to go get my coffee, I'm hiring you to help you to become a competent engineer and become a value creating member of my team. So please don't tell me nothing is above you, not even fetching coffee for me.

I've never hired someone souly for saying this, but it definitely doesn't give you any extra points when we're discussing who to hire after the interview.
